01 · Atlas generation

Participant-native reconstructions, group-space atlas

MesoConnect was generated from high-resolution Human Connectome Project 7T diffusion MRI, with anatomically constrained probabilistic tractography performed in participant-native space.

  1. Estimate intensity-normalized white-matter fiber orientation distributions.
  2. Reconstruct each pathway with MRtrix3 iFOD2 and pathway-specific anatomical constraints.
  3. Clean tractograms and perform slice-wise visual quality control.
  4. Convert valid tractograms to binary participant-level voxel maps.
  5. Transform binary maps to FSL MNI152 1 mm space.
  6. Sum participant maps temporarily and divide by the contributing-subject count to form overlap proportions.
  7. Threshold the overlap-proportion maps at 50% to form the public binary consensus maps.

All seven bilateral pathways were reconstructed in most of the 173 tractography-eligible participants, with 166–173 usable bilateral reconstructions per pathway in the manuscript analysis.

04 · Anatomical constraints

The atlas corridor does not replace anatomy

Each pathway requires seed, target or waypoint, and exclusion logic that reflects its anatomy. The manuscript commands used combinations of the following resources, depending on pathway:

Endpoint and waypoint regions

VTA, anterior or posterior HPC, amygdala, NAc, VP, and fornix.

Broad tissue exclusions

Cortical gray matter, cerebellum, thalamus, and inferior brainstem.

Local anatomical exclusions

Red nucleus, lateral hypothalamus, mammillary bodies, optic structures, or striatum when appropriate.

Laterality

Contralateral hemisphere and contralateral target masks to enforce ipsilateral tracking.

For the VTA–HPC and VTA–amygdala reconstructions, the fornix body was excluded so the streamlines did not follow an unintended fornical route. The Inferior and Superior VTA–NAc pathways used different anterior-commissure exclusions to separate their trajectories.

05 · Applications

Apply the atlas reproducibly

Use pathway-specific anatomy, transforms, tracking parameters, and visual quality control when applying MesoConnect to a new dataset. Record the atlas release and every derived mask with the analysis outputs.

Recommended use: treat MesoConnect as a reproducible, anatomically constrained framework for testing how distinct but interacting mesolimbic pathways relate to cognition, behavior, and disease.

The atlas is software-agnostic. Experienced users may translate its anatomical constraints into MRtrix3, DSI Studio, FSL, DIPY, pyAFQ, or another validated tractography environment. Match the atlas and participant grids, preserve seed/target/waypoint/exclusion logic, inspect laterality and anatomy, and report software-specific algorithms and defaults. The MRtrix3 commands are a transparent reference implementation, not a requirement.
